Lifecycle model

Running, Suspended, Warm, and Cold remain distinct runtime states:

StateProcess memoryHost-bound
RunningActiveNo
SuspendedPreserved; pages may move to local NVMeYes
WarmPreserved in a local VM snapshot; VMM exitsYes
ColdDiscarded; boot from durable disksNo

Suspended and Warm cannot be merged. Both protect Host-local state; Cold starts a new kernel and processes. Leaving Running closes route admission and drains guards before Guest, VMM, blockd, or attachment effects.

Current release boundary

The signed runtime cutover exposes six exact, idempotent product actions: Create, Run, Suspend, Warm, Cold, and Destroy. PostgreSQL admits at most one unresolved operation for a Cat; the assigned Node's signed command runner is the sole physical-effect executor and returns the exact terminal result. Inventory is observation-only and cannot settle an operation. A browser or CLI cannot invent a transition, steal an attachment, or infer Cold from missing local evidence.

There is no product Stop action or Killed state. The Node may produce private execution-stopped evidence while implementing an action, but it is never returned as a product Operation or stable state. Cold requires every retained volume to be remotely durable and released. Missing or corrupt command journal or inventory evidence blocks or quarantines execution. An unplanned cross-Node recovery additionally requires trusted Host-fence and exact Volume takeover authority.
